## EXIT DATASET AND POINT-IN-TIME FEATURES

Every feature is computed from bars at or before the trigger bar's close, and
that close is its recorded `availability_et`. The target — drift from trigger
to session close — is strictly later and is **never** an input.

Trigger definitions, matching how the live bot would act: `reverted` = first
RTH bar whose RSI(14), recomputed with the current price standing in for
today's close, reaches 60; `time_exit` = the first RTH bar; `stop` = the first
RTH bar whose low touches the stop level recovered as
`exit_price / (1 − one_way)`, the exact inverse of `sell_fill`.

## TIME-EXIT MODEL — mechanics, not profitability

**All 238 time exits trigger at `minutes_from_open = 0`.** The condition
`bars_held ≥ 20` is already true when the bell rings; there is no intraday
search. Drift from that trigger to the next tradable print:

| | value |
|---|---|
| mean | **+0.0026%** |
| median | +0.0000% |
| sd | 0.0752% |
| **95% CI** | **[−0.00694%, +0.01288%] — indistinguishable from zero** |

Of the four options the section offers, the evidence selects **(1)
approximately zero execution drift**. This is chosen on observed mechanics: the
decision is executable at the opening print, and the market-order drift from
that print is statistically zero. **The 0.652% charged against these exits is
not an execution cost at all — it prices roughly six and a half hours of
holding between the bell and the close.**

position, or limit-execution probability. **Nothing in H-0013 validates
H-0011's limit behaviour**, and its touch-equals-fill assumption — which

Minimum data that would resolve it: **top-of-book quotes (NBBO) with
exchange-clock timestamps at or finer than the trigger resolution**, covering
the 221 reverted-exit sessions, with quote-condition flags so auction and
crossed states are excludable. Ideally add trade prints with venue and
condition codes to place fills relative to the quote. **Not acquired, and not
to be acquired as a strategy optimisation.**

## CENTRAL QUESTIONS, answered separately

**1. Is +88.3992% a defensible estimate of the frozen strategy's historical
economic performance?**

**No.** It is built from full-sample means — future information relative to
every trade it prices. The honest point-in-time equivalent is **+78.0961%**,
**10.30 points lower**. And even that figure inherits an unstable `reverted`
component: 0.652% sits inside that category's 95% CI, and the point-in-time
estimator is *worse* than the flat constant there. The unstable reverted
component does prevent the conclusion.

**2. How much of the original +58.5889% was suppressed by a demonstrably
incorrect execution accounting convention?**

**About +25 points, and it is the `time_exit` component alone.** Under the
point-in-time model that is **+$25,018 ≈ +25.0 points**; under the full-sample
model +$29,076. The convention is demonstrably wrong there: those exits trigger
at the bell, their market-order execution drift is statistically zero, and they
were charged a 10:00-ET `reverted`-derived number.

**The `reverted` component is not demonstrably incorrect and must not be
counted in that answer.** Its point-in-time reprice is a *debit* of −$4,482,
its confidence interval contains the charged constant, and the PIT estimator
underperforms the incumbent on it.

So: **a defensible correction exists and is worth roughly +25 points, entirely
from one miscategorised bucket. The remaining ~5–10 points H-0012 attributed to
`reverted` is not supported.**

## LIMITATIONS

- **Queue and limit fills** — unresolved, see above.
- **Full-sample vs point-in-time** — model B is disqualified precisely for
  this; C and D never use an exit dated on or after the one they price.
- **Path held fixed** — sizing and slot competition cannot respond. Isolates
  accounting from portfolio path; not what a re-run would give.
- **Impact-audit match rates of 2–35%** — the H-0009 and H-0010 figures lean
  heavily on the pooled fallback. Directions are consistent across two
  independent yardsticks; magnitudes are not established.
- **`rvol_before` covers 126 of 221** reverted exits; 43% fall back.
- **3 stop exits unreconstructed** — gapped through with no intraday touch.
- **Vendor conventions** — RTH 09:30–16:00 ET, DST rule implemented explicitly
  (`tzdata` absent); daily close is the auction print, ~2bp above the last RTH
  bar; RTH volume ≈ 0.891 of daily.
- **Acquisition limit** — the data plan refuses "recent SIP data" for an end
  date of today or later. Does not touch the research window.
- **Sharpe and daily drawdown not recomputed** — a path-fixed reprice cannot
  produce an honest daily mark-to-market curve.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE NEXT STEP

Per the section-11 instruction: the yardstick **can** be partially stabilised,
and the stable part is `time_exit`. But the aggregate error improvement is
**1.3% relative**, and `reverted` — the larger and more interesting category —
resists explanation by every point-in-time feature available, including the one
that survived the stability screen.

**Accounting work on `reverted` should stop.** Further precision there requires
quote-level data, which is the same requirement H-0011's limit-exit question
already generated. That is now the single converged data bottleneck, and it is
a capability question, not another accounting experiment.
