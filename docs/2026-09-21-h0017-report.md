## 11. Queue uncertainty — stated, not minimised

**190 of 221 exits (86.0%) remain queue-unresolved.** NBBO shows displayed
top-of-book size only. It does not reveal the book behind it, hidden liquidity,
order-arrival position, or whether orders ahead consumed the displayed size.

What the evidence *does* constrain: in those 190 cases the median qualifying
volume transacting at or through the level was **8,260× the position's own
share count**. That does not establish a fill — but it does mean a
no-fill outcome would require the hypothetical order to have been behind
thousands of times its own size in queue for an entire session.

**The 28 NO-OBSERVED-TRADE cases are not a bound — they are resolved.** No
qualifying trade occurred at or above the level at any point in the working
session, so no fill was possible regardless of queue. This is the one part of
the H-0011 question the data answer definitively.

Their composition is informative: 13 had limits at/below the bid, 13 at/above
the ask, 2 inside — i.e. they are **not** concentrated in far-from-market
limits. They are sessions where price gapped away and never returned. That is
precisely the adverse-selection mechanism H-0011's own report postulated
("you fill when price rose into your offer and miss when it fell away"),
now measured at **12.7%**.

**Q4 — Execution-model implication.** The evidence **materially narrows** the

- **A definitive negative for 12.7%.** H-0011's daily-bar model treated a

- **An asymmetric constraint for 86.0%.** Median 8,260× volume coverage makes

**A corroboration worth recording, carefully.** H-0011 configuration A
(patience 1 — the same window measured here) modelled a **88.0%** fill rate
from daily bars. The tick-level data show **87.4%** of exits had qualifying

agree to 0.6 percentage points.

That is evidence the daily-bar *touch* approximation was close on the question
it was actually answering. **It is not evidence that the fills occurred**, and
it does not revisit H-0011's verdict — H-0011 was classified B for reasons
beyond the fill assumption (the −$19,062 knock-on, 96.4% of quantities

## 14. Limitations

- NBBO is **top-of-book only**; full depth is unavailable.
- **Queue position is unavailable** and cannot be inferred.
- Displayed size is **not** guaranteed fillable size, and was not used to
  override the volume bound.
- Transacted volume **does not establish this order's execution**.
- Historical quotes and trades describe observed market behaviour; they do not
  recreate a hypothetical order's place in queue.
- 3 trade pulls remain page-capped (all third 3, all already HIGH-BOUND;
  truncation can only understate their ratio).
- 1 quote pull page-capped; spread state for that target is a sample of the
  window.
- 64.3% of raw prints excluded by the registered condition policy, dominated by
  odd lots. A different policy would produce a different bound; this one is
  conservative and was fixed before results.
- **The bands are not probabilities and were not converted into any.**

## 16. Next-step gate

Under **A**, the registered rule is: **do not modify H-0011**, and identify
whether the evidence justifies *one* separately sealed economic experiment
using only the newly established execution fact.

**The newly established fact is narrow and specific:** for a resting sell limit
at the trigger close over the following session, ~12.7% of occasions saw no
qualifying trade at the level at all, and this rate is stable across thirds.

**My assessment is that this does not yet justify an economic experiment**, for
one reason: it corroborates rather than contradicts H-0011's fill modelling
(88.0% vs 87.4%). Re-running H-0011's economics with a 12.7% no-fill rate would
reproduce approximately what H-0011 already produced — and H-0011 was rejected
on grounds this measurement does not address.

**What would justify one** is resolving the remaining 86.0%. That requires
depth-of-book or order-level data — not more NBBO. Specifically: full-depth
book snapshots or MBO/order-level messages with exchange timestamps, which
would allow queue position to be modelled rather than assumed.

## 17. Three things kept separate, as required

**What the market data proves.** 12.7% of hypothetical limits had no qualifying
trade at their level and could not have filled. Quoted spread at these decision
points is 1.63 bps median. 52% of the limits were at or below the bid. All
stable across thirds.

**What the market data cannot prove.** Whether any of the 190 HIGH-BOUND orders
would actually have filled. Queue position, hidden liquidity and depth are
unobservable in NBBO, and no amount of transacted volume resolves them in
principle.

**What remains an economic question requiring a separate seal.** Whether any
execution change built on this has SPY-relative value. Nothing here tests that,
and H-0011's rejection stands on grounds untouched by this measurement.
