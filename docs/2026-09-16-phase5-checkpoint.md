## C. INFORMATION-BOUNDARY ANALYSIS

### The rule, and the timezone conversion

Decision time is **15:45 America/New_York**, the live loop's entry window,
converted to UTC with the daylight-saving rule that applied in that year —
the post-2007 rule from March's second Sunday, and the pre-2007 rule from
April's first Sunday, because the thirty-year window reaches back to 1996.
Visibility: `published_at < decision_timestamp` **and**
`published_at >= decision_timestamp - 72h`. Strictly less than; an item
stamped at the decision instant is excluded.

### The two candidates, and how they were really chosen

You asked whether they were selected because they looked good in the data.
**Yes. Both were.**

1. **Minimum ATR fraction.** The frozen rule caps ATR at 3.5% and has no
   floor, so a name moving 0.6% a day carries a 2.5-ATR stop about 1.5%
   away — inside ordinary noise.
2. **Trend margin above the 200-day average.** `close > SMA200` is a
   binary, so an entry a hairline above it is treated identically to one
   well above.

**The honest sequence:** I ran the anatomy, read split tables that
**contain outcome columns** — median R, stop rate, P&L per bucket — and
picked the two buckets with the worst numbers. The mechanisms above were
articulated *afterwards* to explain what I had already seen. They were not
pre-registered, the bucket edges were chosen by me, and the whole selection
is post-hoc on the development set.

The ATR-floor mechanism happens also to be derivable a priori from the
rule's construction without looking at any outcome. That does not change
how it was actually selected, and I am not going to present it as if it
does.

**They have not been tested further since your instruction, and results
already in hand are reported below only because they were measured before
it arrived.**
