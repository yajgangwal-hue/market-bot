# H-0007 — volatility-percentile entry abstention

*Sealed `02fb9461…` at 2026-09-17T17:32:42Z against commit `5cd6b7ef`,
before any configuration was run. Executed the same afternoon.*

**Verdict: REJECTED.** Not marginally, and not for lack of an effect.
The pre-registered direction was that abstaining more should raise
risk-adjusted return. Across all three sealed cutoffs it lowered it,
monotonically, at Spearman(cutoff, Sharpe) = **−1.0000** where POSITIVE
was declared. The hypothesis was wrong in sign, which is the most useful
way for it to have been wrong.

---

## A. WHAT WAS TESTED

For session *t*, SPY's 20-day realised volatility as of the **prior**
close *t−1* was ranked against every such reading from the start of the
series through *t−1* — an expanding percentile, never a full-sample
quantile. Below the cutoff, no new entries opened on session *t*.

Applied through `model_veto`, which can only **remove** a candidate.
Sizing, stops, exits and the 20-bar holding cap were untouched, and open
positions were unaffected. Before 252 readings existed the percentile
was not trusted and entries were allowed.

Three cutoffs, one family: **A** = 1/3, **B** = 0.25, **C** = 0.20.

### The mechanical prior, and why it was plausible

A mean-reversion edge is the size of the bounce off an oversold reading,
and bounce size scales with volatility. On a calm tape the same RSI ≤ 35
threshold fires on smaller dislocations, so the edge per trade should
shrink toward the cost of trading. That reasoning was formed from the
rule's mechanics, not from an outcome table.

### The search cost, stated

The volatility dimension was **chosen after** a descriptive pass over six
regime dimensions on this same decade. That is recorded in the seal as
provenance. This result carries a multiple-comparison penalty and may not
be read as though the dimension had been named in advance. The penalty
cuts one way here — it would have discounted a pass. It does not soften
a failure.

## E. THE ONE CUTOFF THAT PASSED A AND B IS A SPIKE

C 0.20 gained +0.003240 of Sharpe. Three things disqualify it, and all
three were named in the registration before the number existed.

**It is inside noise.** The standard error of a Sharpe estimate over
10.65 years at this level is ≈0.326 (Lo 2002, iid approximation). The
gain is **0.99% of one standard error**.

**Its ratio rose because the denominator shrank.** Sharpe = mean(daily) /
pstdev(daily) × √252, so mean(daily) = Sharpe × annualised vol / 252:

| | mean daily return | vs baseline | annualised vol | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.00018921 | — | 9.3372% | — |
| C 0.20 | 0.00018604 | **−1.67%** | 9.1232% | −2.29% |

It earns less and risks less. The registration named this in advance: "A
Sharpe rise caused purely by removing exposure is NOT evidence that
selection improved."

**Its neighbours fail.** A lone working cutoff with failing neighbours is
a spike, recorded as one, not a finding.

## F. WHY IT FAILED — THE MECHANISM

### Abstention refused net winners at every cutoff

| config | refused trades | winners | losers | winner P&L | loser P&L | **net refused** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C 0.20 | 125 | 70 | 55 | $53,811 | −$39,344 | **+$14,467** |
| B 0.25 | 153 | 83 | 70 | $63,119 | −$52,990 | **+$10,129** |
| A 1/3 | 252 | 122 | 130 | $101,939 | −$98,779 | **+$3,160** |

Every cutoff declined trades that, taken together, made money. The prior
predicted the opposite.

### Selection per dollar got *worse* as abstention strengthened

| | market | selection | timing+costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.8290% | **0.2068%** | −0.5365% |
| C 0.20 | 0.8786% | 0.2231% | −0.5581% |
| B 0.25 | 0.9055% | 0.1664% | −0.5700% |
| A 1/3 | 0.9927% | **0.0575%** | −0.5459% |

This is the decisive diagnostic. If the prior were right — calm sessions
holding the weak trades — removing calm sessions would **raise**
selection per dollar. At the strongest cutoff it falls to **0.0575%,
under a third of baseline**. On this decade, a calm tape is where this
strategy's entries work *best*, not worst.

### It does not touch the actual drag

The read-only entry attribution found the problem is **timing**, not
selection: selection contributes +$23,836 while timing costs −$61,842.
Abstention leaves timing per dollar essentially where it was (−0.5365% →
−0.5581%). It removes capital from the strategy without addressing what
is losing the money.

## G. WHAT THIS BUYS

A negative result with a **sign**, which is worth more than a null.

1. **Volatility-state abstention is closed as a direction** for this
   strategy, in all three tested strengths, with the gradient measured
   rather than assumed.
2. **The mechanical prior is contradicted, not merely unsupported.** The
   bounce-scales-with-volatility reasoning predicted the opposite of what
   happened. Any future hypothesis resting on that premise now has to
   answer this table first.
3. **Selection is not the problem and abstention cannot fix timing.**
   This is the second independent measurement pointing at entry/exit
   timing as the drag.
4. **The 110% drawdown ceiling did real work.** Two of three cutoffs
   breached it. Without it, "B quartile reduces volatility" could have
   been written up as a risk improvement while drawdown went the wrong
   way.

## I. WHAT COMES NEXT

Three measurements now agree that **timing, not selection, is where the
money goes**: the entry attribution (−$61,842 timing against +$23,836
selection), the regime forensics, and now H-0007's finding that timing
per dollar is invariant to which sessions the strategy sits out.

Selection is positive and abstention makes it worse. Removing trades is
the wrong lever. The next hypothesis should act on the trades the
strategy already takes, at the point where the loss occurs — which the
attribution localises to the gap between the buy-and-hold return of the
chosen name over the holding window and what the strategy actually
realised from it.

That is registered before it is run, as H-0008.
