### Why this was worth asking

`portfolio.py:226-254` charges the **worst** of three trigger-time point
estimates measured on 155 exits — 10:00 ET 0.652%, 12:30 ET 0.289%,
15:00 ET 0.120% — and the file's own comment says the live distribution
is unverified at three logged rule exits, instructing replacement "once
~30 live rule exits have accrued". A worst-case point estimate standing
in for a distribution is a placeholder by construction. The timing
decomposition put $55,405 on it, so it was worth measuring rather than
assuming.

## The reconstruction

The live bot decides on daily bars whose last element is **today's
still-forming bar**, so its close is the current price; `should_exit`
orders stop → RSI≥60 (`reverted`) → cap (`time`). So:

- **`reverted`** — walk the session's five-minute bars forward, forming a
  provisional daily series (completed closes through t−1 plus that bar's
  close), and **halt at the first bar where RSI(14) ≥ 60**. No later bar
  can move the trigger.
- **`time_exit`** — the cap has no intraday component; it is already true
  at the bell, so the trigger is the first regular-session bar.

Drift = `session_close / trigger_price − 1`. The position is long and
being sold, so **positive drift means the close was above the trigger**:
the live bot sold lower than the simulator assumes, and the simulator
overstates by exactly that. The haircut corrects this quantity, so it is
calibrated on the positive tail.

**Coverage: 160 of 459 rule exits (35%).** Not reconstructed: 174 exit
dates outside the intraday window, 122 symbols absent from the cache,
3 that never crossed 60 intraday. The cache is 98 of 230 symbols over
2023-05-22 → 2026-09-15, so this measures the recent window and the
larger names.

## The thing I must not do, stated plainly

**The mean drift is +0.2129% — well below the 0.652% charged.** A
haircut set to the mean would raise decade return mechanically by
roughly $35,000.

H-0008 registered a **percentile of the adverse tail**, not the mean,
and did so before seeing any number, because `portfolio.py` calls the
haircut "a deliberate over-statement correction… a conservative bound,
not a best guess". Switching to the mean now, having seen that the mean
is favourable, is exactly the move pre-registration exists to prevent.

The mean is recorded in the ledger and in
`docs/phase5/h0008-adjudication.json` under
`mean_drift_recorded_not_adopted`, and a test pins that it was recorded
and not adopted.

Whether the haircut should **bound** a cost or **estimate** one is a
real and unresolved question. It is a legitimate future registration —
with the acceptance criteria written first — and it is not a conclusion
of H-0008.

## The ceiling on this whole exercise

The simulator decides on completed daily closes. The live bot can cross
RSI 60 intraday on a day that closes back below 60, exit, and leave the
simulator still holding.

| | |
|---|---|
| held sessions inside the intraday window | 2,461 |
| of those, closing below RSI 60 | 2,457 |
| of those, crossing RSI 60 **intraday** | **46 (1.9%)** |

On those days the live bot exits and the simulator does not. **No
haircut at any value represents that divergence** — it is a model gap,
not a parameter. 1.9% is small, and now measured rather than assumed.

## Causality

The measured effect is **execution assumptions only**. No entry
selection, exit selection, exposure, beta, cost or risk-normalisation
change is involved, because nothing traded differently. The result
adjusts confidence in the simulator's accounting and adjusts nothing
else. Reported performance is unchanged because the haircut is
unchanged.

## Decision

**REJECTED.**

The registered direction was contradicted in sign. The existing haircut
stands unchanged, and at the bounding percentiles the evidence suggests
it understates rather than overstates the cost — which is the safe
direction for the error to point.
