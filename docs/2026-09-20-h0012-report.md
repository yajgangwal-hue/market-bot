## REPRICING MODEL — exact, deterministic, sealed in advance

The simulator records `exit_raw = close × (1 − H)`. The measured trigger price
is `close / (1 + A)`. Setting them equal gives **`H' = A / (1 + A)`**:

| reason | measured A | H′ | multiplier `(1−H′)/(1−H)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reverted` | +0.611300% | +0.607586% | **1.00044706** |
| `time_exit` | −0.110900% | **−0.111023%** (a credit) | **1.00768031** |
| `stop`, all others | — | unchanged | 1.0 |

Applied analytically to the frozen trade log. `net_pnl = (exit_price′ −
entry_price) × quantity` — verified an **exact identity, max error 0.0000000000
across all 698 trades**, before sealing. Mean used, not median, registered in
advance because P&L aggregates additively in price. **Neither population is
extrapolated onto the other; no full-sample average replaces reason-specific
behaviour.**

**Equivalence gate: baseline reproduced at +58.5889000000% / 698 trades before
any repricing was computed.**

## WHY THE VERDICT IS C — and a correction to my own interim claim

Mid-run I wrote that "`reverted` drives only 5.7% of the repricing." That is
true at the sealed point estimate and **misleading as a stability statement.**
`reverted`'s share is small *only because* its full-sample mean (+0.6113%)
sits almost exactly on the charged 0.652%, so the multiplier is near 1. Move
the estimate and its contribution explodes.

| varied input | repriced delta range | **spread** |
|---|---|---|
| **`reverted` thirds** | +17.25 … **+53.63** pts | **36.39 pts** |
| `time_exit` thirds | +26.87 … +34.53 pts | 7.66 pts |
| worst third for both simultaneously | +49.66 pts | — |
| point-in-time estimates | +30.76 pts | — |
| median instead of mean | +29.03 pts | — |

**`reverted` is the larger instability, not the smaller one.** Choosing which
third you believe moves the answer by 36 points.

**What is robustly established — the direction.** Every single variant is
positive and clears the 2-point penalty by more than an order of magnitude.
The old haircut was materially too conservative, and that is not in doubt.

**What is not established — the magnitude.** +17.25 to +53.63 points depending
on the estimation window. A single historical constant replacement is therefore
**not defensible**, which is exactly the condition the seal named. Verdict C.

## LIMITATIONS

- **Queue and limit fills.** 5-minute OHLCV cannot reconstruct an order book,
  depth, quoted spread or queue position. H-0012 touches **only market-order
  exit categories, whose mechanics are measurable from the available data.**
  H-0011's limit-exit fragility is **not addressed and remains unresolved.**
- **Full-sample vs point-in-time.** The repricing is a *historical descriptive
  estimate*, in-sample by construction. It is not a point-in-time executable
  model and no simulated trader acts on it. The expanding-window cross-check is
  reported above rather than assumed away.
- **Path held fixed.** Sizing and slot competition are not allowed to respond.
  The H-0011 gate showed that response is real (quantity differed on 96.4% of
  trades). This isolates the accounting effect — the right decomposition for a
  yardstick audit — but it is **not** what a full re-run would produce.
- **Vendor conventions.** RTH filter 09:30–16:00 ET with the DST rule
  implemented explicitly (`tzdata` is not installed here). Daily close is the
  closing-auction print; the last RTH bar is not — a ~2bp systematic
  difference. RTH volume is 0.891 of daily.
- **Sharpe and daily drawdown not recomputed**, for the reason given above.

## What this does NOT establish

It does **not** show the strategy is better, that anyone could capture the
trigger-to-close movement, that a new exit rule is profitable, that intraday
trading works, or that a lower haircut means higher real-world returns. It
establishes only that **the historical emulator's execution accounting was
materially conservative, and specifically miscategorised.**

## What must now go to separately governed review

1. **H-0009's ordering change** (B/C swap) — verdict unaffected, ordering moved.
2. **H-0010 configuration A's sign flip** (+18.58 → −5.39) — strengthens the
   existing rejection; no reopening required, but the record should say so.
3. **Whether `time_exit` should ever have carried a `reverted`-derived
   haircut** — the 94.3% finding. This is a design question about the
   instrument, not an economic experiment.

## What additional data verdict C requires

More rule-exit observations **per reason per period**, so a within-window mean
has usable precision — 65–99 observations per reason per third is not enough
against sd of 1.8–2.1%. Alternatively a conditioning model (time-of-day,
volatility) that *explains* the third-to-third variation rather than averaging
it. Until one of those exists, no single constant should replace 0.652%.
