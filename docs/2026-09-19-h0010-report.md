# H-0010 — stop width and a 2.5R take profit

*Sealed `cff28642…` at 2026-09-19T22:48:11Z against commit `66a79bce`,
before any configuration in the family was run. Adjudicated the same
day.*

**Decision: REJECTED** on clauses B, C and E. The frozen 2.5-ATR stop
and the absence of a take profit both stand. **Nothing in production
was changed at any point.**

---

## What was asked, and what was done

You asked for a 2% stop, a 5% take profit, and a ~2-day maximum hold.
All three were **measured before anything was set**:

| | total return | Sharpe | max drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | +58.59% | 0.5107 | −12.98% |
| hold 2 days only | **−77.52%** | −2.3933 | −77.89% |
| stop ~2% only | +30.46% | 0.2810 | −26.71% |
| **stop ~2% + TP 5%** | **+77.84%** | **0.6040** | −17.47% |
| all three as asked | **−78.57%** | −1.8493 | **−79.67%** |

**The 2-session holding cap was excluded on that evidence and never
registered.** It converts 1,747 of 1,821 trades into forced time-exits
and collapses the `reverted` bucket — the only profitable one in the
frozen strategy, +$248,916 — from 221 trades to 5.

The stop-plus-take-profit result looked like the best finding of the
whole program, so it was sealed as a proper three-point family rather
than adopted.

## The family

Take profit fixed at **2.5R** throughout; stop width is the gradient.
A literal fixed 2% stop is **not expressible in configuration** — the
stop is `multiple × ATR(14)`, spanning 2.99%–7.73% of price — so
0.917 × ATR is the closest approximation, centring the average stop
near 2%. An exact fixed 2% would need a production edit and was not
made.

## Clause E — the clause this experiment was built around

A take profit is a **resting limit**. `portfolio.py` fills it at its
level, or at the open on a gap, and — unlike `reverted` and
`time_exit` — **it pays no 0.652% rule-exit haircut.**

| config | gain vs baseline | haircut avoided | **gain NET of it** | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A stop 0.917 | **+$19,246** | **$58,857** | **−$39,611** | **3.06×** |
| B stop 1.25 | −$4,472 | $34,683 | −$39,156 | — |
| C stop 1.75 | −$20,354 | $9,189 | −$29,543 | — |

**The avoided haircut is 3.06× the entire gain.** Strip the execution
advantage and configuration A is **$39,611 worse** than the baseline.

The gradient confirms the mechanism rather than leaving it an
inference. As the stop widens:

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| take-profit exits | 335 | 213 | 67 |
| haircut avoided | $58,857 | $34,683 | $9,189 |
| return delta | +19.25 | −4.47 | −20.35 |

All three move together. **The apparent improvement was a resting
limit escaping a cost that market exits pay — not better selection.**

That is legitimate modelling, not a bug: a real limit order genuinely
does escape trigger-to-close drift. But it is an *execution* finding,
and it says nothing about whether tighter stops and profit targets
pick better trades.

## What this leaves behind

The registered expectation was that A would fail on drawdown. It did,
and it failed on two further clauses that the seal was specifically
written to catch. The sealing order mattered: had the +19.25 points
been adopted on sight, the strategy would have taken a 3.19-point
drawdown breach in exchange for an execution artefact.

**One genuinely useful finding survives, and it is not a trading rule.**
Limit exits avoid the 0.652% haircut that market exits pay, and across
987 trades that was worth **$58,857** — comfortably larger than any
strategy effect measured in this program. That points at *how orders
are placed*, not at what to trade. It belongs with the execution work
H-0008 opened, and it would need its own registration.
