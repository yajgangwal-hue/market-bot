# Review and changes — 2026-08-28

## 1. Starting point: there were no trades to review

The request was to review recent trades on the $1,000 demo account and analyse
the unprofitable ones. That review could not be run as written, because:

- `data/` contained only `.gitkeep`. There was no journal, no trade log, no
  order history.
- There was no demo account. The package had no brokerage integration at all —
  `journal.py` opens with *"no broker or order-routing code exists"*, and a
  search for broker/order/account code returned only comments saying so.
- Running the bot's own backtest over two years of real SPY data produced
  **zero trades**, in-sample and out-of-sample.

So there were no unprofitable trades, and also no profitable ones. Rather than
report "all clear", the question became *why* nothing had ever traded. The
answer was four defects, described below. Everything after this point is
measured on real market data (7 ETFs, 501 daily bars each, Yahoo Finance).

## 2. Defect 1 — a $1,000 account could not buy the universe

`position_size` floored the share count to a whole number. At the default 0.5%
risk, a $1,000 account has a **$5.00** risk budget, while one share of SPY with
a 2-ATR stop risks about **$16.76**. `floor(5 / 16.76)` is `0`, so the candidate
was rejected — for a reason having nothing to do with the quality of the setup.

| Symbol | Entry | Risk/share | Shares @ $1k (before) | Shares @ $1k (after) |
|--------|-------|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| SPY | 640.00 | 16.76 | **0** | 0.298357 |
| QQQ | 570.00 | 16.67 | **0** | 0.299860 |
| GLD | 315.00 | 8.37 | **0** | 0.597143 |
| XLK | 260.00 | 8.31 | **0** | 0.601887 |
| XLE | 88.00 | 3.10 | 1 | 1.612903 |
| XLF | 52.00 | 1.56 | 3 | 3.202049 |
| TLT | 88.00 | 2.10 | 2 | 2.380952 |

Four of the seven symbols were structurally untradeable. Across 2,037
symbol-days, **55% of all rejections** were this one rule.

**Change.** `RiskPolicy` gained `allow_fractional_shares` (default `True`),
`fractional_decimals`, and `min_notional`. `position_size` returns a float and
rounds **down**, so realised risk is still exactly $5.00 — the risk percentage
is unchanged, only the granularity is. Every broker this project targets for
paper trading supports fractional orders. Set `allow_fractional_shares=False`
to restore whole-share behaviour.

*Result: a $1,000 account now sizes identically to a $100,000 one.*

## 3. Defect 2 — six stacked hard filters left a 0.15% pass rate

`strategy.py` documents a deliberate split: **blockers** are "things known to
destroy accounts", **score** "ranks survivors". In practice three checks were
doing both jobs at once:

| Feature | Hard blocker | Also a scored component | Score scale floor |
|---------|-------------|------------------------|------------------|
| relative volume | `< 1.20` → reject | `participation`, 10 pts | **1.20** |
| trend fit R² | `< 0.40` → reject | `trend_quality`, 13 pts | **0.40** |
| 5-bar momentum | `<= 0` → reject | `momentum`, 13 pts | **0.0** |

Each component's scale *started at its own blocker threshold*, so it could never
observe a low value — 36 of 100 points that could only ever score high. This is
precisely the "dead weight" the `ScoreComponent` docstring warns about. ANDing
six roughly-50% filters left **2 candidates in 2,037 symbol-days (0.10%)**.

**Change.** These three are preferences, not the illiquidity and cost failures
that actually destroy an account, so by default they now *rank* a survivor
instead of rejecting it, and their score scales were widened to cover the newly
admissible range (momentum now starts at −2%, since a shallow pullback inside an
intact uptrend is a normal entry). Three config flags —
`strict_participation_gate`, `strict_momentum_gate`, `strict_trend_fit_gate` —
restore the old hard gates individually.

The genuine capital-destroyers are untouched and still hard blockers: price
floor, dollar-volume liquidity, ATR band, `price > MA20 > MA50`, ADX, ATR
extension, event conflict, cost-versus-expected-move, net reward-to-risk, and
every risk guard.

*Result: 2 → 23 candidates (0.10% → 1.13%). 583 symbol-days now reach the
scoring stage instead of being hard-rejected, which is how the design intended
the score to be used.*

## 4. Defect 3 — a NaN bar crashed the live path

Every one of the seven downloaded CSVs ended with a row like:

```
2026-08-28T16:00:00-04:00,57.97999954,58.40999985,57.91999817,nan,25049523
```

That is the session still in progress; Yahoo returns its close as `NaN`. The
existing guard was `if any(value is None ...)`, and `float('nan') is not None`,
so it passed straight through. Running the bot today raised:

```
ValueError: cannot convert float NaN to integer
```

This was **pre-existing**, not introduced by the sizing change — the original
committed `position_size` called `floor()` on the same NaN. Worse than the
crash: in a backtest a NaN reached `net_pnl` and silently produced NaN P&L,
which propagates through the equity curve and every summary statistic.

**Change.** `data.py` now screens NaN, infinity, and non-positive prices in both
`load_bars` and `fetch_yahoo_bars`, with the reasoning recorded at the guard.
Bad rows are skipped rather than crashing mid-calculation.

*Result: the live path runs. SPY today loads 500 clean bars and returns a
legitimate `REJECT` — "ADX 15.3 is below 18.0" — instead of a stack trace.*

## 5. Defect 4 — the holding period was a magic number deciding most exits

The backtest closed positions at `min(index + 5, last)`, a literal `5` buried in
the loop. It decided **7 of 10** exits.

**Change.** It is now `StrategyConfig.max_holding_bars`, default unchanged at 5.

The default was **not** re-tuned, and that is deliberate. Sweeping it with a
proper 70/30 chronological split gave:

| hold | in-sample net | in-sample avg R | out-of-sample net | out-of-sample avg R |
|------|--------------|----------------|------------------|--------------------|
| 5 | $9.20 | 0.369 | $3.32 | 0.133 |
| 10 | $18.02 | 0.721 | $0.11 | 0.005 |
| 15 | $16.81 | 0.841 | −$2.77 | −0.138 |
| 20 | $18.23 | 0.912 | $7.76 | 0.517 |
| 30 | $18.25 | 0.913 | $8.68 | 0.579 |

In-sample points at 20–30; out-of-sample disagrees at 10–15 and agrees at 20–30.
Each cell rests on **three to five trades**. That is not enough to distinguish a
parameter from noise, and picking the best row would be exactly the overfitting
the project's own README warns against. The parameter is now named and testable;
choosing it needs far more data.

## 6. The trades themselves

With the defects fixed, the same rules over the same two years on $1,000 produce
10 trades — the first trade record this project has ever had.

| # | Symbol | Entry | Exit | R | Net $ | Exit reason |
|---|--------|-------|------|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | GLD | 2025-04-04 | 2025-04-04 | −0.96 | **−4.78** | stop |
| 2 | XLK | 2025-06-30 | 2025-07-07 | +0.16 | +0.82 | time |
| 3 | XLK | 2025-07-10 | 2025-07-16 | −0.01 | **−0.04** | time |
| 4 | GLD | 2025-10-10 | 2025-10-16 | +1.91 | +9.51 | target |
| 5 | GLD | 2026-01-13 | 2026-01-20 | +0.73 | +3.69 | time |
| 6 | XLE | 2026-03-03 | 2026-03-09 | −0.41 | **−2.04** | time |
| 7 | XLE | 2026-03-17 | 2026-03-23 | +0.48 | +2.42 | time |
| 8 | XLE | 2026-03-25 | 2026-03-31 | +0.42 | +2.10 | time |
| 9 | QQQ | 2026-05-28 | 2026-06-03 | +0.59 | +2.96 | time |
| 10 | XLF | 2026-07-16 | 2026-07-22 | −0.42 | **−2.12** | time |

Net **+$12.52 on $1,000 (+1.25%)** over two years. 6 wins / 4 losses.
Costs $1.92, which is 15% of net profit.

### The four unprofitable trades

**#1 GLD, −$4.78, stopped out the same day.** The only full stop-loss. Entered
after gold had run, and the 2-ATR stop sat inside one day's range.

**#3 XLK, −$0.04, flat.** Entered, went nowhere, closed at the 5-day clock. The
loss is essentially the spread. Not a thesis failure — a trade that should not
have been worth opening.

**#6 XLE −$2.04 and #10 XLF −$2.12, both time exits.** Both drifted against the
entry and were closed by the clock, not by the stop.

### What the losers have in common

Three of the four lost to the **clock**, not to the stop. And the structural
problem is visible across all ten: **only one trade in ten reached its target**,
while seven were closed by time. The rule risks 1R to reach a 2R target, but in
practice collects roughly +0.4R when the clock runs out. Risking one unit to
collect four tenths is a losing geometry no win rate fixes cheaply.

That is the single most actionable finding, and the fix is *not* to widen the
holding period on this evidence (§5 shows why). The honest options, in order of
how testable they are:

1. **Match the target to the window.** A 2R target on a 2-ATR stop needs roughly
   a 4-ATR move; at ~1% daily ATR that is a 4% move in 5 sessions. Rare. Either
   the target comes down or the window goes up — but the choice needs enough
   trades to measure.
2. **Trail the stop** so a time exit banks more of a favourable excursion.
   `Trade` already records `max_favorable_excursion_r`, so this is measurable
   against the existing record.
3. **Screen out trades like #3** — a setup whose expected move barely clears
   costs. The cost gate exists but is loose relative to a 5-day hold.

None of these should be adopted on ten trades. They are the hypotheses worth
collecting data on.

## 7. Information-source audit

The bot has no Twitter/X ingestion running (`social.py` requires a pinned,
verified allow-list that is not configured, and a post can never set a trade
direction by design — that is sound). The defects were in the **headline
classifier**, which does feed `IMPACT_MAP` and therefore trade scoring.

`_terms_in` used plain substring matching. Observed, not hypothetical:

| Headline | Was classified | Because | Now |
|----------|---------------|---------|-----|
| "Apple unveils new software features" | **geopolitics / risk_off** | `war` inside "soft**war**e" | other / neutral |
| "Warehouse automation startup raises funding" | **geopolitics / risk_off** | `war` inside "**War**ehouse" | other / neutral |
| "Shipping costs ease across Asian routes" | **inflation** | `ppi` inside "shi**ppi**ng" | other / neutral |
| "FedEx cuts full-year guidance" | **central_bank** | `fed` inside "**Fed**Ex" | earnings |

`geopolitics/risk_off` maps to SPY −0.40, QQQ −0.35, GLD +0.45. A routine Apple
product headline was pushing the bot away from equities and toward gold.

**Contradictory signal.** `"above forecast"` appeared in **both** the `hawkish`
and `risk_on` term lists. The winner was decided by dictionary iteration order,
not by evidence. The phrase is genuinely category-dependent: above-forecast
*inflation* is hawkish (bearish equities); above-forecast *payrolls* is risk-on.

**Changes.**
- Whole-word regex matching, compiled and LRU-cached (a screening run classifies
  the same terms thousands of times).
- `"above/below forecast"` moved out of the fixed lists into `SURPRISE_STANCE`,
  keyed by `(category, direction)` — so the same phrase resolves to `hawkish`
  for CPI and `risk_on` for payrolls, which it now does.
- An unresolved tie between opposing stances is no longer decided silently: the
  stance is forced to `neutral`, confidence capped at 0.2, and the note is
  prefixed `CONTRADICTORY:` for review.

## 8. A real demo account

Added `broker.py`, an adapter for a real **Alpaca paper account** — real
symbols, real market data, real order types and rejections, simulated money.
`event-aware-trader account` shows balance, positions, and recent orders.

Safety properties, each covered by a test:

- **The live endpoint is refused outright.** `_require_paper_endpoint` raises on
  anything but `paper-api.alpaca.markets`, so a typo in an env var cannot route
  an order to real money.
- **Credentials come only from the environment**, never a file, so a key cannot
  be committed by accident.
- **Two independent flags** must both be cleared to send anything: the config
  needs `allow_order_submission=True` *and* the call needs `dry_run=False`. Both
  default safe.
- **Fractional orders are handled honestly.** Alpaca does not accept a bracket
  order for a fractional quantity, so for those the entry is a plain market
  order and the stop/target are returned flagged as *locally managed and not
  resting at the broker*. Silently dropping them would be the dangerous thing.

You create the account and export the keys; this code will not do either:

```bash
export APCA_API_KEY_ID=...
export APCA_API_SECRET_KEY=...
event-aware-trader account
```

## 9. What was deliberately not changed

- **The risk percentage.** 0.5% per trade, 1.5% daily, 6% weekly stay as they
  are. Fractional sizing solved the $1,000 problem without loosening risk.
- **The long-only, liquid-ETF universe**, and every hard blocker in §3's last
  paragraph.
- **Human review before any order.** A candidate is still `PAPER_LONG`, not a
  buy instruction.
- **`minimum_score = 70`**, untouched — with the gates rebalanced it is now
  doing real ranking work (583 reach scoring, 23 clear 70).

## 10. Honest limits

- **This is not a day-trading system, and cannot become one by tuning.** It
  reads *daily* bars and holds ~5 days. Day trading needs intraday data, an
  intraday execution path, and a cost model for far more frequent turnover.
  Every number here is from daily bars. Note also that a US margin account under
  $25,000 is subject to the pattern-day-trader rule, which limits day trades to
  three in five business days. *(Correction, 2026-10-04: the SEC approved the
  rule's removal on 2026-04-14, effective 2026-06-04, with brokers phasing it
  in by 2027-10-20. See `2026-10-04-day-trading-everything-else.md` §3.7.)*
- **Ten trades prove nothing.** +1.25% over two years is indistinguishable from
  zero at this sample size, and it underperformed simply holding the index. The
  value delivered here is that the bot now produces a measurable record at all.
- **The backtest still flatters itself**: no market impact, no halts, no
  delistings, no taxes, survivorship-clean ETFs, and conservative-but-estimated
  6bp slippage.
- **No change here creates an edge.** They remove defects that made the bot
  inert, crash-prone, and occasionally wrong about what a headline said. What
  the strategy is worth is still an open question, and the way to answer it is a
  large sample of paper trades on the Alpaca account.
