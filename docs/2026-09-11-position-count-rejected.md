# The better entry does not buy a wider book

**11 September 2026.** The account owner wants the bot working harder — more
trades, more of the account deployed. The thing standing in the way is the
200-day trend filter: with it on the bot holds about 3 positions and sits 77%
in cash; with it off it holds 6.

He removed it on 2026-09-09 for exactly that reason, and put it back on
2026-09-10 after one broadly red session showed him what six correlated longs
and $703 of cash feels like from the inside.

That decision was made against a measurement taken with the **old entry
timing**, where every position was bought the morning after the signal.
Entering at the signal's own close is worth +1.3 CAGR points *and improved
drawdown* — the opposite of how return is usually bought here — so the
trade-off was worth re-measuring rather than assuming unchanged.

## Decade

| | total | CAGR | maxDD | 1st half | 2nd half | trades | worst year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| filter ON, open entry (old) | +123.1% | 7.83% | −14.1% | +66.1% | +34.3% | 710 | 2022 −7.3% |
| **filter ON, close entry (shipped)** | **+153.9%** | **9.14%** | **−13.7%** | +68.3% | +50.8% | 713 | 2022 −8.8% |
| filter OFF, open entry | +157.4% | 9.28% | −22.4% | +76.4% | +45.9% | 1,117 | 2022 −13.4% |
| filter OFF, close entry | +175.1% | 9.97% | −18.5% | +54.3% | +78.3% | 1,117 | 2018 −3.3% |

The decade alone makes a real case for removing it: +0.83 CAGR points for 4.8
points of drawdown.

**But the decade contains no sustained bear market, and the filter's entire
purpose is what happens in one.** Nothing ships on it.

## Thirty years, two bear markets

| | total | CAGR | maxDD | 1st half | 2nd half | trades | worst year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **filter ON, close entry** | +443.7% | 5.68% | **−14.2%** | +39.8% | +288.8% | 1,525 | 2022 −10.1% |
| filter OFF, close entry | +700.5% | 7.03% | **−33.4%** | +43.8% | +456.6% | 2,772 | **2008 −22.6%** |

+1.35 CAGR points, bought with **19.2 more points of maximum drawdown** and a
2008 that goes from −10.1% to −22.6%.

That is the same trade as before, in the same direction and roughly the same
size. The better entry did not change it, because the two things are
independent: the entry timing improves the price of each trade, and the filter
decides whether the trade is taken at all during a decline. The mechanism is
unchanged — in a sustained fall almost nothing is above its 200-day average, so
with the filter on the rule stops buying and sits in cash, and with it off it
keeps buying dips all the way down.

## Verdict

**Filter stays on.** The owner's own call on 2026-09-10 was right, and the
thirty-year numbers back it rather than merely permitting it.

The way to give him more return was never the filter. It was the entry clock,
which delivered +1.3 points on the decade and +1.1 over thirty years with the
drawdown *improving* — nearly as much return as removing the filter, without
any of the 19 points of pain.
