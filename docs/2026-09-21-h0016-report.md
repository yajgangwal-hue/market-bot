## 1. CLASSIFICATION: **C — CASH CONSTRAINT IS NOT ECONOMICALLY IMPORTANT**

Cash binds **mechanically and often** — 839 of 1,539 production-evaluated
candidates, 54.52%. But the candidates it rejects are **worse than the ones the
frozen system accepts** on the strategy's own objective, at every horizon, and
the pattern **fails the preregistered 2-of-3 stability rule**.

Not **B** ("value unclear"): the evidence is not merely weak or noisy — it
points positively the other way. Not **A**. Not **D** for the candidate-level
question, though portfolio-level attribution is separately unidentifiable (§11).

`entry_fill="signal_close"` makes the pending-path capacity counter at

### A reconstruction error I found and fixed before reporting

Available cash was reconstructed as `day_start_cash − outlays accepted earlier
that session`, validated against production's own counter. **The first attempt
reproduced only 598 of 839 and the run stopped itself rather than reporting.**

Cause: `position_size` runs **before** the conviction multiplier (up to 1.5×)
and the `max_notional` ceiling clamp, so its return is *not* what production
spends. Hooking `cap_by_participation` — which receives the fully scaled,
clamped quantity immediately before the fill and cash test — gives the size
that actually sets outlay. After the fix: **839 reconstructed = 839 counted,
exact match.**

### Why raw return and excess return disagree — the decisive mechanism

Cash-denied candidates have *higher raw* forward returns (1.3378% vs 0.8507%
at 10 sessions) and *worse excess* returns. Implied SPY return over each
population's own windows:

| | 1 | 5 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| over accepted windows | −0.0162% | +0.2636% | **+0.5782%** | +1.0042% |
| over cash-denied windows | +0.0209% | +0.7404% | **+1.3924%** | +2.4876% |

**Cash runs out precisely when the bot is already heavily invested, and it is
heavily invested after the market has been rising.** Cash-denied candidates are
therefore clustered in stronger market windows — SPY returns ~1.39% over their
10-session windows against ~0.58% over the accepted ones. Their higher raw
return is **market exposure they happened to sit beside, not selection
quality.** This is exactly the §19 falsification condition *"the opportunity
disappears after SPY-relative comparison."*

MFE is reported descriptively and is **not** converted to hypothetical profit:
6.0980% denied vs 6.1694% accepted — essentially identical, while MAE is
*shallower* for denied (−3.8132% vs −4.9078%).

## 11. COUNTERFACTUAL ATTRIBUTION TEST — **not identifiable**

The seal required identifiability to be established *before* running anything,
and predicted this outcome in advance.

**Attribution is not identifiable.** Admitting a previously rejected candidate
would consume cash, occupy a correlation bucket and take a position slot,
changing the state every subsequent decision observes — different guard
outcomes, different cash, different candidates. The delta of such a run is a
*joint* effect of cash plus bucket plus slot plus ordering, and no unique
cash-attributable component can be extracted from it.

**Per the seal, the counterfactual was NOT run.** Reporting the ambiguity is
the registered result; manufacturing attribution is forbidden.

This is moot in any case: Claim 2 fails, so there is no established opportunity
for a counterfactual to convert into portfolio value.

## 12. DOES CASH SUPPRESS ECONOMICALLY VALUABLE OPPORTUNITY?

**No, on this evidence.** The four claims, kept separate as registered:

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| **1 — Cash binds mechanically** | **ESTABLISHED.** 839/1,539 = 54.52%, directly counted |
| **2 — Cash-denied candidates hold value** | **FAILS.** −0.0546% SPY-relative at 10 sessions, and −0.3271% *worse* than accepted; the higher raw return is market beta |
| **3 — Stability** | **FAILS.** 1 of 3 thirds; 30.3% of denials in 2024 |
| **4 — Portfolio-level economic impact** | **NOT IDENTIFIABLE**, and unsupported regardless since 2 fails |

A frequent constraint is not a valuable constraint to remove. **The frozen
sequence is admitting the better candidates by the strategy's own objective** —
cash is discarding the weaker ones, and it is doing so as a side effect of
being fully invested rather than by any selection logic.

meaningful economic bottleneck**. That is done.
