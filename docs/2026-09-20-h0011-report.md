# H-0011 — resting-limit exits, the day-trading research's one reachable lead

*Sealed `eeab1b43…` at 2026-09-20T03:20:01Z against commit `22f2752`,
before any configuration was run. Adjudicated the same day.*

**Decision: INCONCLUSIVE.** All five clauses pass on all three
configurations, by wide margins — and the result does not survive the
uncertainty the seal itself required to be priced. **Nothing in
production was changed at any point.**

---

## Why this experiment exists

The three day-trading surveys of 2026-09-19 closed that direction on seven
independent lines, but identified one mechanism that is both the top-1%
discriminator and reachable here: **the profitable minority is paid the
spread rather than paying it.** Sharpe scales as (edge/noise) × √N, and this
bot's edge/noise of 0.07760 only becomes a large Sharpe once cost per trade
is near zero. That is step one of a dependency chain, and nothing else in the
chain works without it.

H-0010 had already measured the same thing from the inside — $58,857 of
haircut avoided because take-profit exits are resting limits — and flagged
that it **priced only the benefit.** H-0011 prices both sides.

## The mechanism

`mr_limit_exit = (offset, patience)`, default `None`. When the RSI exit fires,
the position is **not** sold; a resting sell limit is armed at the trigger
close and works for `patience` sessions from the next session. Fill at the
open if the open is at or above the level, else at the level if the high
reaches it, else spend a session of patience. On exhaustion — or on reaching
the 20-bar cap — sell at the close and pay the 0.652% haircut, recorded as
`limit_lapsed`. Offset fixed at 0.0; patience is the gradient.

## A run was discarded

**The first run reached bar 21 and breached the 20-bar holding cap.** When the
RSI exit fired *on* bar 20, the code armed a limit instead of crossing, and the
order worked into bar 21. The sealed rule says the cap stays hard, so that run
did not implement its own registration. It was discarded (kept at
`docs/phase5/h0011-cache-DISCARDED-cap-breach.json`), the arming condition was
fixed to fall through to the market exit on the last permitted bar, and all
three configurations were re-run. Every number below is from the corrected run,
in which the cap is respected everywhere.

## Why INCONCLUSIVE

### 1. The queue assumption carries almost the entire result

On daily bars, a bar whose high touches the level is assumed to fill a resting
order. A touch is not a fill — you sit in a queue behind everything already
resting at that price, and a level touched once and rejected is exactly where
the queue does not clear. The seal required this to be **disclosed and not
netted out**.

Splitting the fills by whether queue position can matter:

| config | gap fills (no queue risk) | intrabar touches (queue decides) | % at risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 108 | 52 | 32.5% |
| B | 110 | 56 | 33.7% |
| C | 112 | 59 | 34.5% |

A **gap fill** is unambiguous: the market opened above the offer and printed
through it. An **intrabar touch** needs price to rise *into* a resting order,
and that is where the assumption does work it may not be entitled to.

Charging every intrabar touch as a non-fill:

| config | headline clause E | **queue-adverse floor** | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | +$28,012 | **+$2,997** | pass, by $997 |
| B | +$27,767 | **−$2,909** | **FAIL** |
| C | +$30,827 | **+$168** | **FAIL** |

**About 90% of the gain evaporates, one configuration goes negative, and the
ordering inverts.** A gradient that reverses under its own disclosed
uncertainty is the same defect clause C exists to catch, arriving by a
different route. The seal says a pass by a margin smaller than the disclosed
uncertainty is inconclusive. This is that case.

### 2. Most of the gain is added exposure, not execution

This is the more serious finding, and it is a defect in the clause I wrote
rather than in the result. Decomposing the filled-trade gain into the haircut
it escaped and the price move it captured by holding one more session:

| config | total gain | escaped haircut | **price move** | **% is move** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | +$51,459 | +$21,077 | **+$30,382** | **59%** |
| B | +$51,286 | +$21,650 | +$29,636 | 58% |
| C | +$51,945 | +$22,328 | +$29,617 | 57% |

**Fifty-seven to fifty-nine percent of the gain is price move.** That is not
execution. It is one extra session of market exposure — and Part I measured
that **82% of this market's return accrues overnight**, so holding one more
night is close to the most reliable way to manufacture return in this dataset
without improving anything.

Exposure confirms it: 44.28% → 45.14–45.34%.

Clause E was written to separate execution from **selection**. It does not
separate execution from **added exposure**, and this program has been caught by
exactly that before — the standing note that the SPY gap is exposure, and that
exposure breaks the risk budget. A mechanism that earns its return by being in
the market longer must be adjudicated as a risk change, not an execution change.

## What is actually established

**Something real is here, and it is smaller than the headline.**

- **The escaped haircut is genuine and consistent: $21,077–$22,328 across
  176–185 filled exits.** A resting limit that fills does not pay the
  trigger-to-close drift that a market order pays. This is the same mechanism
  H-0010 found and the same one the microstructure literature says separates
  the top of the intraday distribution.
- **Gap fills are unimpeachable.** 108–112 of them, where the market opened
  through the offer. No queue argument touches those.
- **The cost side behaves exactly as registered.** Lapses lose money, more
  patience costs return, and the fill rate rises with patience. The model is
  not flattering itself.

**What is not established** is that any of this clears the bar once queue
position is priced and the exposure component is removed. Strip both and the
defensible core is roughly the escaped haircut on gap fills alone — on the
order of **$13,000–$14,000 over a decade, about 13 points of terminal return**,
with wide uncertainty and no way to validate the fill assumption from daily
bars at all.

## What would settle it

Three things, none available today:

1. **Intraday or order-book data** to replace the touch-equals-fill assumption
   with a measured fill probability conditional on queue position.
2. **A separate registration that holds exposure constant**, so the execution
   effect is isolated from the extra overnight session. This is the single most
   important follow-up and it is a clause-design fix, not a new idea.
3. **Real resting-order fill data from the live account.** The 0.652% haircut
   rests on 155 exits and three logged rule exits; the fill assumption rests on
   nothing. Both need live measurement.
